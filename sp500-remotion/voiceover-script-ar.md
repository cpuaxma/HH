## Pronunciation (where AI voices usually go wrong)

| Written | Why it's written this way | Listen for |
|---|---|---|
| خَمْسُمِئَة | The digits «٥٠٠» are often read as "five hundred" in English, or as «خمسة صفر صفر». | khamsu-mi'a. If the voice still says «خمسة مية», try «خَمْسُ مِئَة» (two words). |
| أكبر خَمْسِمِئَةِ شركة | After «أكبر», the number takes kasra (خَمْسِمِئَةِ). | Fine either way in natural speech. The tashkeel just stops a strange stress. |
| إس آند بي خَمْسُمِئَة | Pasting the Latin "S&P 500" makes many voices switch to English ("es and pee five hundred") or drop the «&». | Arabic accent all the way through, with no switch to English. |
| ألفٍ وتِسْعِمِئَةٍ وسبعةٍ وخمسين | Digits «١٩٥٧» can be read one digit at a time, or as «ألف وتسعمئة وسبعة وخمسون». | It must end in «خمسين», not «خمسون» (it follows «عامِ»). |
| نحوَ عشرةٍ بالمئة | The symbols «≈» and «٪» are skipped or read as "approximately" and "percent" in English. | «نحوَ» for ≈ and «بالمئة» for ٪. Never paste the symbols. |
| صِحَّة | Without the shadda, the voice may say «صَحَّ» or «صِحَه». | siḥ-ḥa, with a clear doubled ḥ. |
| المُؤَشِّر | Without the shadda, the voice may say «المؤْشَر». | al-mu'ash-shir. |
| يُتابِعُه | Without tashkeel, the voice may say «يَتابَعَه». | yu-tā-bi-'uh. |
| رقمٍ واحد | This tells the voice it means "a single number". | A short pause after «شركة...», then «في رقمٍ واحد». |
| تيليجرام | It matches the spelling on screen. | Some voices say "Telegram" with a hard g (Egyptian style). Either is fine. Just don't let it switch to English. |
| ... | Most voices make a short dramatic pause at «...». | If one reads it as «نقطة نقطة» or rushes through, replace «...» with «،». |

## Making sure the AI doesn't change the wording

- **Turn off rewriting:** disable any "enhance", "improve script", "rewrite" or auto-translate option in the voice tool. It must read the text as pasted.
- **Set the language:** choose Arabic explicitly. Don't leave it on auto-detect, or the Latin-looking words may flip it to English.
- **Generate one line at a time:** use the paste blocks above. Short clips keep the wording exact and are easy to line up with the "Starts at" times.
- **Check by ear:** every word you hear should match the "On screen" column. The only exceptions are numbers and symbols read out as words, and the Version B additions.
- **Delivery:** confident, warm, documentary-style Modern Standard Arabic. Faster and punchier on lines 1–2 (the hook), a calmer explainer pace from line 3, and slow and friendly on line 10.

## Grammar note on line 10

On screen, «تابعنا» is singular but «وانضموا» is plural. In a voiceover the switch is easier to hear than to read. If you'd like it to match, use «تابعونا للمزيد...» or «وانضمّ إلى قناتنا...». Then change the screen text in `src/config.ts` (`TEXT.cta`) too, so the two stay the same.

## Putting it in the video

Line the clips up on a 33.0-second timeline at the "Starts at" times. `voiceover-guide.srt` has these lines and times, and you can import it into Premiere, CapCut, DaVinci and so on as a guide track. Export the result as **one file**, `public/voiceover.mp3`, starting at 0:00.0. Then render again:

```bash
npx remotion render SP500 out/sp500.mp4 --codec=h264 --crf=18
```

When a voiceover is present, the music (if any) is lowered automatically.
